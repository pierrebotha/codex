The smell of aviation fuel. Always the fuel. It clung to Pieter van der Merwe’s flight suit like a second skin, acrid and inescapable. He slumped into the canvas chair outside the briefing tent, the rough fabric scratching against his exhausted shoulders. Above him, the sky over southern Angola was a bruised, metallic grey. It didn't look like a sky that belonged to them anymore.

'You're shaking, man,' Coetzee muttered, tossing a dented aluminium water bottle onto Pieter's lap.

Pieter stared at his hands. A faint, uncontrollable tremor in his fingers. 'Just... it's the adrenaline. Or the heat.'

'Right.' Coetzee didn't sound convinced.

The war had shifted. It wasn't the sudden, violent ambushes of the early days, where the South African Air Force would strike hard and vanish before the dust settled. Now? Now the air tasted of radar lock-ons and sophisticated surface-to-air missiles. The Cuban and Angolan defences had grown thick, a dense web woven across the border.

'Did you see them?' Pieter asked, unscrewing the cap. The water was lukewarm and tasted of rust.

'The MiGs?' Coetzee rubbed the back of his neck, leaving a streak of oil across his sunburnt skin. 'Briefly. They didn't engage. Just... watching.'

Watching. Waiting. Pieter closed his eyes, the roar of his Mirage F1’s Atar engine still echoing in his skull. His last sortie had been a nightmare of evasive manoeuvres, throwing the heavy fighter through the sky to dodge a missile that had come entirely too close. The balance of power, once held so confidently by the SAAF, was tilting. He could feel it in the air pressure. He could see it in the exhausted, hollowed eyes of the ground crews.

'They're getting bolder,' Pieter said softly. A fragmented thought flashed through his mind. *The warning receiver screaming. The sudden plunge. Survival.*

'We'll adapt,' Coetzee offered. It sounded like an empty promise.

Pieter looked back up at the bruised sky. They would have to. Or they wouldn't survive the year.

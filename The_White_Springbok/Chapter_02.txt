Dust. It was the first thing Pieter remembered about his first deployment. Red, pervasive dust that settled in the corners of his eyes and ground between his teeth. The memory surged forward, unbidden, as he sat in the suffocating heat of the debriefing room.

'You broke formation, van der Merwe.' The commandant's voice had been cold, devoid of the camaraderie they shared in the mess hall.

'I saw an opening, sir.' The younger Pieter had stood rigid, sweat trickling down his spine, soaking the cotton of his shirt. 'I thought I could hit the convoy before they dispersed.'

'You thought.' The commandant leaned forward, the creak of his leather chair loud in the silent room. 'You are not here to be a hero. This isn't a bloody film.'

The words still stung, years later. *Discipline.* That was the lesson carved into him during those early, chaotic strikes deep inside Angolan territory. The Bush War didn't reward mavericks; it killed them.

'Remember the brief,' the old voice echoed in his mind. *Check your six. Trust your wingman. Conserve fuel.*

He had learned to suppress the urge to chase every target. To pull back on the stick when his blood roared for pursuit. Survival wasn't about the kill; it was about bringing the aircraft back to base. The rough grip of the throttle, the precise, almost mathematical calculation of speed and altitude—these were his anchors.

'We don't fly on luck,' he murmured to himself, staring at the scarred wooden table.

The bush forgave nothing. One mistake, one moment of misplaced bravado, and the dense, unforgiving canopy below would swallow a downed pilot without a trace. Discipline was the only armour they had.

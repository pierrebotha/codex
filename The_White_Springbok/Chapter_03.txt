The radar scope glowed with a sickly, pale green light, casting long shadows across the ops room. Major Visser stood over the plotting table, a stubby pencil tapping rhythmically against the map of southern Angola. *Tap. Tap. Tap.* The sound grated on Pieter’s frayed nerves.

'They're not just conscripts anymore,' Visser said, his voice tight. 'Intelligence confirms it.'

Pieter leaned closer, the harsh glare of the overhead bulb highlighting the deep lines etched around his eyes. 'Cubans?'

'Experienced ones.' Visser circled a coordinate near Cuito Cuanavale. 'Flying MiG-23s. The Floggers are here, Pieter. And they brought the new toys.'

The room seemed to shrink. The MiG-23 was a completely different beast compared to the older, slower airframes they were used to swatting from the sky. Swept wings, brutal acceleration, and most terrifyingly, advanced radar and beyond-visual-range missiles.

'So, we're outgunned,' Coetzee said from the corner, arms crossed tightly over his chest.

'Out-ranged, certainly,' Visser corrected, though the distinction offered little comfort.

Pieter felt a cold knot tighten in his stomach. The technological superiority the SAAF had leaned on, the edge that had kept them alive, was gone. It had evaporated in the space of a few weeks.

'They're flying aggressive patrols,' Visser continued, sliding a grainy reconnaissance photograph across the table. 'Coordinated. Disciplined. They're trying to push us out of the sector entirely.'

Pieter picked up the photo. It showed the sleek, predatory silhouette of a MiG on a runway. *A new game.* The rules had changed, and the stakes were higher than ever. The skies over the bush were no longer their exclusive hunting ground. They were prey now, too.

The name drifted through the humid air of the mess tent like a contagion. Whispered between sips of warm beer, muttered during pre-flight checks, it carried a weight that made even the most seasoned pilots pause.

'Alejandro Ruiz,' Coetzee said, swirling the dregs of his drink. 'That's what the intercept transcripts call him.'

Pieter wiped condensation from his glass, the wet glass slippery under his thumb. 'Just a name.'

'More than a name, *boet*.' A pilot from another squadron leaned over from the next table, his voice low. 'They say he's an ace. Shot down two Unita choppers and chased a pair of Impalas all the way back to the border. He flies a MiG with a black eagle painted on the tail.'

*A black eagle.* Pieter pictured it against the harsh glare of the African sun. A symbol of dominance.

'Propaganda,' Pieter said, though his own voice lacked conviction. He’d seen the shift in tactics. The precision. The ruthless efficiency of the enemy formations. Someone was leading them, someone who understood aerial warfare on an intimate, terrifying level.

'Our intel blokes think he's an instructor,' Coetzee added. 'Brought in to train the Angolans, but he’s taking to the air himself. Hunting us.'

The tent was stifling, the smell of burnt paraffin and cheap tobacco heavy in the air. Yet, Pieter felt a sudden chill. It wasn't just a faceless enemy anymore. It was a man, a specific pilot with a reputation that was rapidly turning into a legend among the communist forces.

'Let him come,' Pieter said quietly, staring at the scarred wooden table. *Let him come.* But beneath the bravado, he felt the sharp prick of anticipation. The sky was about to become very crowded.

Major Alejandro Ruiz stepped onto the baking tarmac of the Menongue airbase, the heat radiating through the thin soles of his boots. The air tasted of dust and aviation fuel—a familiar cocktail, whether in Havana, Moscow, or here, at the edge of the world.

He walked slowly around his MiG-23, running a gloved hand along the leading edge of the wing. The metal was hot, vibrating with a latent, violent energy. On the tail, the black eagle emblem glared out at the scrubland.

'She is ready, Major,' the chief mechanic called down from the cockpit ladder, a smear of grease across his cheek.

'Thank you, Carlos.' Ruiz’s voice was calm, measured. He didn’t shout. He didn’t need to.

He climbed the ladder, settling into the cramped, utilitarian cockpit. The smell of old rubber and ozone grounded him. He flipped a series of switches, watching the dials flicker to life with quiet satisfaction. Discipline. It was the only thing that mattered.

The South Africans were skilled, he conceded that. They flew their Mirages with a desperate, aggressive flair. But they were intruders. They didn't belong in this sky. They were a temporary obstacle in a much larger historical struggle.

*They fight for a dying idea,* he thought, adjusting his oxygen mask. *We fight for the future.*

He felt a profound, fierce patriotism burning in his chest. It wasn't hatred that drove him, but a cold, absolute certainty in his cause. The South Africans would be driven back. It was inevitable. And he, the Black Eagle, would be the instrument of their defeat. He closed the canopy, sealing himself away from the African heat, ready to hunt.

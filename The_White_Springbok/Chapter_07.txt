Back on the ground, the silence of the ops room felt heavy, almost oppressive. Pieter stared at the flight recorder data printed on a long strip of paper, the erratic lines tracing a battle that felt entirely unreal in retrospect.

'He matched you turn for turn,' Visser said quietly, leaning over Pieter's shoulder.

'He anticipated me,' Pieter corrected, rubbing his aching neck. 'Every manoeuvre. It was like he knew what I was going to do before I did.'

In his quarters at Menongue, Alejandro Ruiz sat on his narrow cot, a cup of strong, black coffee growing cold on the small table beside him. His flight suit lay discarded on the floor. He stared at his hands. They were steady, but his mind raced, replaying the engagement over and over.

*The South African.* The pilot of the Mirage. He hadn't panicked when the radar lock was established. He hadn't broken and run when the MiG engaged. He had fought back with a calculated, ferocious skill that Ruiz had not encountered since his training days in the Soviet Union.

'He is good,' Ruiz murmured to the empty room.

He felt a strange, unsettling sensation in his chest. It wasn't fear. It was respect. A professional acknowledgement of a peer. The propaganda painted the South Africans as cowardly mercenaries, relying on superior equipment rather than skill. The man in the Mirage was no coward.

'Next time,' Ruiz said softly, picking up the cold coffee. 'Next time.'

Miles away, Pieter lay awake, listening to the distant hum of a generator. He knew, with a sudden, absolute clarity, that their paths would cross again. The sky felt smaller now. Too small for both of them.

The smell of fresh paint mingled with the pervasive stench of aviation fuel. Pieter walked out to his Mirage just as the sun began to blister the horizon. Two ground crewmen were standing back, admiring their work beneath the cockpit canopy.

'What's this, then?' Pieter asked, running a finger over the crisp white silhouette.

'A springbok, sir,' the younger mechanic said, grinning. 'For luck. The way you threw this bird around yesterday... we figured you needed a proper emblem. Something to answer that Cuban’s eagle.'

Pieter stared at the crisp white lines against the camouflage paint. A White Springbok. It was audacious. A deliberate, visible challenge. It wasn't camouflage; it was a target painted directly on him.

'He sees this, he'll know it's you,' Coetzee said, coming up behind him. 'You're making it personal.'

'It's already personal,' Pieter replied quietly.

He climbed into the cockpit, the leather seat familiar and comforting. The symbol changed nothing about the aircraft's performance, but it changed everything about how he felt sitting in it. It was a statement of defiance. A refusal to be intimidated by the legend of the Black Eagle.

When he fired up the Atar engine, the roar seemed louder, more resonant. He wasn't just another faceless SAAF pilot anymore. He was the White Springbok, and he was hunting. As the Mirage tore down the runway and clawed its way into the sky, Pieter felt a cold, clear focus settle over him. He was ready.

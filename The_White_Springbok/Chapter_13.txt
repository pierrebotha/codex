Ruiz stormed across the apron at Menongue, his face dark with fury. The MiG-23 shuddered as it powered down, but Ruiz didn't wait. He practically leapt from the cockpit ladder, marching straight toward the chief mechanic, Carlos.

'Major?' Carlos asked, taking a step back at the look in Ruiz's eyes.

'The hydraulic pressure dropped during the climb!' Ruiz shouted, his voice cracking like a whip across the noisy flight line. 'The starboard flap actuator was sluggish. Do you want me to die up there, Carlos? Is that it?'

'We... we replaced the seals yesterday, Major. Parts are scarce—'

'I don't care about parts!' Ruiz slammed his fist against the fuselage, ignoring the heat of the metal. 'I care about surviving! He had me, Carlos. The South African had me. If his missile hadn't failed, I would be a smear on the ground.'

The mechanics stood in stunned silence. The Black Eagle never lost his temper. He was the embodiment of cold, communist discipline. To see him rage was deeply unsettling.

Ruiz turned away, breathing heavily, running a trembling hand through his sweat-soaked hair. The anger was a mask. Beneath it, a cold, creeping dread was taking root. He had made a mistake today. He had let the red mist of the hunt blind him to his surroundings, and the White Springbok had capitalised on it instantly.

He walked toward the briefing hut, the adrenaline slowly ebbing away, leaving him hollow. His luck was running out. He could feel it in the sluggish response of the stick, in the weary groan of the airframe. The sky was no longer a hunting ground; it was a trap waiting to spring.

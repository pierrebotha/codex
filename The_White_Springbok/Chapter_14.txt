The briefing room at the SAAF base was suffocatingly hot, the air thick with the smell of cheap coffee and nervous sweat. Major Visser stood before the map board, a long wooden pointer tapping against a red circle near Cuito Cuanavale.

'FAPLA is moving a massive armored column south,' Visser said, his voice flat and serious. 'They intend to break the stalemate. Intelligence indicates FAPA will commit everything they have to provide air cover.'

Pieter sat in the front row, staring at the map. The lines and circles blurred together.

'This is the big one, gentlemen,' Visser continued. 'We're sending a full strike package. Mirages, Buccaneers, Impalas. Your job is to keep the MiGs off the mud-movers. It’s going to be crowded, and it’s going to be bloody.'

Coetzee leaned over. 'He'll be there.'

'I know,' Pieter replied, his voice barely a whisper.

He felt a strange sense of calm wash over him. The months of skirmishes, the desperate chases, the misfires—it had all been leading to this. The grand, chaotic theatre of a major offensive.

He walked out to the flight line as the dawn painted the sky in bruised shades of purple and orange. The ground crews were working frantically, loading ordnance, fueling the thirsty jets. He stood before his Mirage, running a hand over the painted White Springbok.

This was the last sortie. He could feel it in his bones. The tension that had coiled tight in his chest for months was finally ready to snap. Today, the sky would demand a resolution. He climbed into the cockpit, the canopy sealing him in, and prepared to face the Eagle.

The sky over Cuito Cuanavale was a chaotic tapestry of contrails, exploding flak, and the frantic radio chatter of a dozen separate engagements. Pieter threw the Mirage through a violent barrel roll to avoid a stream of tracer fire, his breath coming in ragged gasps against the G-force.

Then, he saw it. The distinctive, sweeping profile of a MiG-23, cutting through the furball with predatory grace. The black eagle on the tail was unmistakably clear in the harsh sunlight.

Ruiz saw him at the same moment. As if tied by an invisible thread, both aircraft broke away from the main battle, dragging their private war away from the chaos and out into the vast, empty expanse of the southern Angolan sky.

The duel began.

It was a masterclass in aerial combat, an exhausting, high-speed chess match played out in three dimensions. Ruiz used his massive thrust to try and drag the fight into the vertical, booming down and zooming away, trying to bleed Pieter’s energy. But Pieter refused to play the Cuban’s game. He stayed low, hugging the deck, forcing Ruiz to dive into the thick, drag-heavy air where the Mirage’s agility reigned supreme.

They scissored, they rolled, they pulled bone-crushing Gs, each trying to find that split-second advantage. Sweat poured into Pieter’s eyes, stinging sharply. His muscles screamed in protest, but he didn't yield an inch.

Ruiz flashed across his canopy, so close Pieter could see the rivets on the fuselage. Pieter hauled the stick back, trying to bring his guns to bear, the roar of his Atar engine echoing the pounding of his own heart. Neither man would break. Neither man would run. The sky was not big enough for them both.

The silence that followed was broken only by the distant pop of cooling metal from the ruined MiG. The adrenaline crash hit them both simultaneously, a heavy, leaden weight that pressed them into the dirt.

Pieter sat up slowly, wincing at the throb in his jaw. He looked around. Dense, impenetrable bush stretched in every direction, a harsh landscape of thorn trees, scrub, and unforgiving red earth.

'We're deep,' Pieter said, his voice hoarse. 'Miles behind the lines.'

Ruiz remained silent, staring at the smouldering wreckage of his aircraft. The symbol of his power, his connection to the Soviet war machine, was gone.

'Nobody is coming for us immediately,' Pieter continued, pulling out his compass. 'The airspace is too hot. They won't risk a Chopper until things quiet down.'

'So, we are prisoners of the bush,' Ruiz finally spoke, his English heavily accented but precise.

'Something like that.' Pieter carefully checked his survival kit. A knife, matches, a half-empty canteen, some rudimentary medical supplies. He glanced at Ruiz. 'What do you have?'

Ruiz hesitated, then patted his pockets. 'A Makarov. One magazine. No water.'

The reality of their situation starkly illuminated the absurdity of their recent brawl. The ideologies, the flags, the painted symbols on their aircraft—none of it mattered here. The bush didn't care if they were communist or capitalist. It only cared if they were weak.

'If we stay here, we die,' Pieter said, standing up and brushing the dirt from his legs. 'FAPLA patrols will investigate the smoke. Or the heat will get us.' He pointed a finger south. 'That way lies my lines.'

Ruiz stared at him, a complex mix of hatred, pride, and pragmatic realisation warring in his eyes. He slowly stood up, wincing as he clutched his ribs.

'Then we walk south,' Ruiz said quietly.

They didn't shake hands. There was no truce declared. But as they turned their backs on the burning wreckage and stepped into the suffocating embrace of the bush, an unspoken understanding hung between them. Survival had become the only mission that mattered.

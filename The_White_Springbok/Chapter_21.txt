The heat was a physical weight, pressing down on them with relentless pressure. By the second day, the animosity between them had burned away, replaced by the grim necessity of putting one foot in front of the other.

Pieter led the way, his instincts honed by years of survival training. He found a muddy seep and showed Ruiz how to filter the water through a cloth, the liquid tasting of earth and desperation.

'Drink slowly,' Pieter rasped, watching the Cuban gulp greedily. 'Or you'll bring it right back up.'

Ruiz nodded tersely, wiping his mouth. His flight suit was torn, his face smeared with grime, the proud Black Eagle reduced to a man trying to survive.

They encountered a herd of elephants later that afternoon. Pieter froze, grabbing Ruiz’s shoulder and forcing him down behind a cluster of thorn bushes. They held their breath as the massive grey shapes moved silently through the scrub, a terrifying reminder of how small they were in this landscape.

'They are majestic,' Ruiz whispered, a rare moment of awe piercing his stoic facade.

'They will crush you without noticing,' Pieter replied softly.

The most terrifying moment came on the third day. They reached a dry riverbed. As Pieter started to step down the bank, he noticed a subtle disturbance in the dirt. A faint, perfectly circular depression.

'Stop!' Pieter hissed, throwing his arm out.

Ruiz froze mid-step.

'Minefield,' Pieter pointed to the faint outline. 'Soviet PMN. They must have seeded this crossing.'

They spent the next hour painstakingly navigating the perimeter, every step calculated, every breath held. The shared terror bonded them in a way their duel never could. They were no longer enemies; they were two men against the absolute indifference of the African bush.

The nights were brutally cold, a stark contrast to the burning days. They huddled beneath a large acacia tree, sharing the meagre warmth of a tiny, smokeless fire Pieter had managed to build in a hollow.

'Why did you come here?' Pieter asked, breaking a long silence. He poked the embers with a stick. 'To Africa.'

Ruiz stared at the small flames. 'Duty. Solidarity. To fight for those who cannot fight for themselves.'

Pieter snorted softly. 'You really believe that?'

'I did,' Ruiz said, his voice quiet. He looked up at the vast, star-strewn sky. 'My father fought at the Bay of Pigs. He told me the revolution was a fire that would cleanse the world. But here... here it feels like we are just burning down the house to kill the rats.'

Pieter nodded slowly. 'We call it fighting terrorists. Protecting our borders.' He tossed a dry twig into the fire. 'But mostly, we just try to keep our mates alive long enough to go home.'

They sat in silence for a while. The ideological walls that had separated them—capitalist and communist, South African and Cuban—felt absurdly flimsy out here.

'You are a good pilot,' Ruiz said suddenly, not looking at him.

'So are you.'

It was the closest they would come to an apology, or a truce. It was an acknowledgement of shared suffering and mutual respect. As they settled down to sleep, the vast emptiness of the bush surrounding them, they both knew that whoever won this war, they would carry this long walk south with them forever.

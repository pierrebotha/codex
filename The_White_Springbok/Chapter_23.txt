The crack of an AK-47 shattered the early morning stillness. Bark exploded off a tree inches from Pieter’s head.

'Down!' Pieter yelled, diving into the thick undergrowth.

Ruiz hit the dirt beside him, pulling the Makarov from his pocket. They had stumbled into a FAPLA patrol—six men, heavily armed, moving in a loose line through the scrub.

'They saw us,' Ruiz whispered, his knuckles white around the pistol grip.

'Stay low,' Pieter ordered. He unholstered his own survival pistol. It was practically useless against assault rifles at this range, but it was all they had.

The patrol advanced cautiously, shouting to each other in Portuguese. Pieter’s mind raced. He knew the terrain better than they did. He knew how to move without being seen.

'Follow me,' Pieter hissed. 'Exactly in my footsteps.'

He began a slow, agonising crawl through the thorn bushes, using every dip and hollow in the ground to stay hidden. He led them in a wide arc, flanking the patrol. Ruiz followed silently, trusting the South African’s instincts completely.

They reached a rocky outcrop just as the patrol reached their former position.

'Now,' Pieter whispered, rising up and firing two quick shots towards the patrol. He didn't aim to hit; he aimed to distract.

The FAPLA soldiers spun around, unleashing a hail of wildly inaccurate fire at the rocks. But Pieter and Ruiz were already moving, sprinting away in the opposite direction, using the confusion to break contact. They ran until their lungs burned, the sounds of pursuit fading into the dense bush behind them. They had survived, together.

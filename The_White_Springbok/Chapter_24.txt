The distant *thwack-thwack-thwack* of rotor blades was the most beautiful sound Pieter had ever heard. It was their fifth day. They were severely dehydrated, exhausted, and barely able to walk.

A Puma helicopter crested the treeline, its camouflage paint a welcome sight. Pieter pulled a signal mirror from his kit and flashed it toward the cockpit.

The chopper banked sharply, descending into a nearby clearing, kicking up a storm of red dust. Heavily armed pathfinders leaped out, weapons raised.

'Van der Merwe?' the lead soldier shouted over the roar of the engines.

'Here,' Pieter rasped, raising a hand.

The soldiers rushed forward, grabbing him under the arms. Then they saw Ruiz. Weapons immediately tracked onto the Cuban.

'Who the hell is this?' the soldier demanded.

'Prisoner of war,' Pieter said loudly, pushing a rifle barrel aside. 'He comes with us.'

Ruiz stood tall, despite his exhaustion. He didn't raise his hands in surrender, nor did he resist as the soldiers patted him down and took his Makarov. He looked at Pieter, a long, unreadable stare.

As they were hauled into the belly of the Puma, the soldiers shoved Ruiz roughly into a corner seat. Pieter sat opposite him. The helicopter lifted off, banking hard toward the border.

Ruiz looked out the window at the receding bush, then back at Pieter. There was no hatred left. Just the bone-deep weariness of a man who had fought a war and lost, but had kept his dignity. Pieter offered a small nod, an acknowledgement of the shared trial they had survived. The war wasn't over, but their private battle was finally at an end.
